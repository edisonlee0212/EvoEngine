## Knowledge Ledgers

Authoritative world state is not any actor's map. Each civilization maintains a ledger of observations and inferences.
Every known object records the observed time, report-arrival time, facts, uncertainty, prediction model, provenance,
trust, secrecy, and access permissions.

Orders obey the same rules. A central order becomes effective only after reaching a capable local authority. Until then,
the destination follows standing policy, local leadership, and its latest valid instructions.

## Exploration and Detection

Exploration progresses from remote astronomy to directed observation, probes, crewed missions, and settlement. Each
method trades cost, delay, information quality, and detectability.

Civilizations create observable signatures through waste heat, radio leakage, propulsion, industrial spectra,
megastructures, and beacons. Detection depends on signature, distance, sensor aperture, observation time, occlusion,
background noise, and signal age.

Silence is costly rather than magical. Tight-beam communication, passive observation, low-emission infrastructure,
camouflage, and distributed industry trade convenience and growth for lower visibility. Large energetic societies cannot
be perfectly invisible.

## Dark-Forest Dynamics

The dark forest is an emergent possibility, not a rule that all intelligence follows. Delayed communication, uncertain
intentions, limited knowledge, and destructive technology create rational fear. Verification, costly signals, trade,
shared institutions, and mutual dependence can create rational cooperation.

The player may broadcast, observe silently, deceive, relocate, share knowledge, conceal capability, build deterrence,
or attack based on incomplete evidence. No label reveals that an unknown civilization is objectively friendly or
hostile.

## Diplomacy Under Delay

Diplomacy consists of timestamped messages, proposals, commitments, and actions. Negotiation across fifty light-years is
not a synchronous conversation. Treaties need local interpretation and mechanisms that remain useful while each side
waits decades for a reply.

Reputation is observer-relative and delayed. A society may praise a reform after it has been reversed or retaliate
against a government that no longer exists.

## Interstellar Travel and Colonization

Default travel is slower than light. Missions have acceleration limits, cruise profiles, mass, energy, reliability,
payload, autonomy, and arrival uncertainty. Infrastructure such as relays, depots, beam stations, habitats, and
industrial nodes turns geography into a constructed network.

A colony is a vulnerable new society, not a color-fill action. It needs viable biology or habitats, equipment, skills,
population, governance, local industry, and a plan for years without support. Founder effects and isolation encourage
cultural divergence, autonomy, failure, or eventual superiority to the founding world.

## Conflict

Conflict includes espionage, information warfare, economic exclusion, proxy struggles, interception, blockade,
habitat seizure, system denial, fleet action, and attacks on worlds. Outcomes derive from prepared capability,
intelligence, geometry, logistics, doctrine, and local autonomy.

There is no instantaneous tactical command across interstellar distance. Fleets execute objectives and rules of
engagement locally. The player shapes doctrine before contact and receives the outcome after delay.

Weapons capable of sterilizing worlds or approaching relativistic strike speeds must be rare, expensive, observable in
some phases, and politically transformative. Their existence should change strategy rather than reduce late play to
routine planet deletion.

## Open Questions

- How can hidden information remain fair when catastrophic attacks may arrive from unknown origins?
- Which late technologies deepen causal strategy rather than merely shrinking travel time?
- How much local battle resolution should be visible or interactive?
- Which trust and verification mechanisms can remain useful across century-long exchanges?

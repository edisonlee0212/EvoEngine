## Civilization Identity

A civilization is represented by a lineage graph rather than a permanent numeric owner. Nodes are political and
cultural organizations; edges record descent, secession, union, conquest, recognition, and inheritance.

The player may continue through constitutional change and choose whether a splinter, successor, diaspora, archive, or
reconstructed society represents continuity. That choice has costs and does not rewrite how other societies perceive
the lineage.

## Population

Population is aggregated into cohorts at settlement or habitat scale. Relevant properties include:

- Size, age structure, fertility, mortality, and migration pressure.
- Biology and environmental tolerances.
- Education, expertise, health, and productivity.
- Culture, language, ideology, allegiance, and institutional trust.
- Material needs, expectations, and exposure to risk.

Exceptional leaders, scientists, diplomats, and symbolic figures may be individual characters, but demographic
simulation does not depend on representing every person.

## Economy and Logistics

The economy tracks strategically distinct stocks and flows rather than every commodity. A useful initial set is energy,
raw mass, rare feedstocks, food and biosphere support, fabrication capacity, compute and communication capacity, skilled
labor, and institutional capacity.

Production requires local inputs, infrastructure, labor, energy, and time. Interstellar trade moves aboard physical
missions and cannot teleport into a global stockpile. The UI may show accounting totals, but shortages resolve locally.

Logistics is the practical boundary of power. A distant fleet without maintenance, replacement parts, reliable orders,
or local legitimacy eventually ceases to function as an extension of its capital.

## Research and Preservation

Research produces theories, designs, methods, and data. Knowledge must be communicated, taught, implemented, and
preserved. Possessing a design does not grant the industry or expertise required to build it.

Archives, universities, redundant repositories, standards, and machine-readable records are survival infrastructure.
Collapse can destroy active capability while leaving enough knowledge for recovery. Secrecy protects discoveries from
rivals but reduces redundancy and increases permanent-loss risk.

## Governance and Administrative Reach

Government is a hierarchy or network of decision nodes. Each has jurisdiction, legitimacy, competence, doctrine, and a
communication relationship with other nodes.

Distance creates an administrative horizon. Beyond it, instructions arrive too late for routine central control. The
player responds through autonomy, federal structures, standing orders, local AI, cultural alignment, prediction, or
coercion. Each approach creates distinct failure modes.

## Stability, Fragmentation, and Collapse

Instability grows from understandable pressures: unmet needs, inequality, legitimacy loss, cultural divergence,
communication delay, defeat, ecological damage, elite conflict, technological disruption, and outside influence.

Collapse occurs at different levels. A government can fall while its population survives; a union can fragment into
functioning states; a world can lose industry but retain culture; a species can disappear while descendants or archives
continue. Recovery and successor formation are normal parts of history.

## Non-Player Decision Structure

AI civilizations use their own delayed knowledge and cannot inspect authoritative remote state. Their behavior derives
from needs, values, risk tolerance, institutions, internal factions, and history.

Decision-making is hierarchical:

- Local controllers handle production, safety, and immediate response.
- System governments coordinate worlds and local traffic.
- Regional institutions allocate major missions and maintain routes.
- Civilizational strategy establishes long-horizon goals and identity.

Quiet regions plan on scheduled intervals. New information, shortage, conflict, or political thresholds increase
decision frequency. Major actions should retain inspectable reasons based on what the actor knew at the time.

## Open Questions

- How much biological and cultural variation is simulated rather than expressed as authored traits?
- Which economic stocks produce useful logistics without inventory bookkeeping?
- How predictable should social change be?
- What makes a successor legitimate for player continuity and legacy reporting?
- Can delegation remain understandable across thousands of settlements?

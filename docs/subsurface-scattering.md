# Subsurface scattering

SSS implementation and validation are in progress. Profiles and transport are opt-in; existing materials have zero SSS weight.

## Authoring

Create a **SubsurfaceProfile** asset with the `.evesss` extension. The profile contains scattering albedo, RGB diffusion distance, and surface IOR. Distances are measured in meters, with one engine world unit interpreted as one meter.

In the material inspector's **Subsurface scattering** section, assign a profile and set weight, distance scale, and geometry mode. The weight mask uses the red channel. Effective weight is clamped after mask sampling and also blends the authored surface IOR toward the profile IOR before Fresnel/refraction evaluation. A zero mask retains the authored surface response; raster, ray BSDF and fast shadow transmission share this rule. Thickness uses a constant in meters multiplied by the red channel of an optional thickness texture. For closed solids this is the minimum/fallback raster transmission thickness; punctual shadow depth and the raster back-face pass can increase it. Solid PT obtains thickness from geometry. Scattering distance and thickness are world-space controls; object scaling changes geometric thickness in PT and the raster back-face pass but does not rescale those authored lengths.

Profiles are shared assets. Editing a profile invalidates dependent material snapshots and rendering history. Material asset references retain profiles for scene serialization and runtime export. A missing profile disables SSS until the asset is available, preserving the authored weight.

- **Closed solid:** homogeneous surface diffusion for a connected, closed mesh. PT samples another surface point on the same render instance using a three-axis projected diffusion sampler.
- **Thin surface:** open leaves/paper with two-sided diffuse reflection/transmission. Authored thickness partitions the diffuse response using an exponential approximation; this mode does not require volume entry/exit. It is an approximation to sheet transport, not a layered fiber or paper microstructure simulation.
- Opaque and alpha-masked raster geometry are the initial targets. Alpha-blended raster SSS is not implemented. Metals have no diffuse SSS contribution.

## Rasterization

Deferred lighting separates diffuse from specular/emission across DDGI, SDFGI, and HDDAGI. A graph-owned RGBA16F diffuse signal is allocated only when SSS is active. The diffusion pass samples a radial two-exponential profile in world units and projects its radius to pixels. It rejects unrelated instances, incompatible profiles, opposite normals, and depth separation before recombining diffuse lighting with the sharp surface response.

Closed solids use HDRP-style thick-object transmission. Point and spot lights estimate the light-to-receiver distance minus the closest shadow-map occluder distance, converting perspective/cube-face depth to radial world distance. The authored thickness sets a minimum: shadow-derived thickness can only reduce transmittance. This attenuation replaces binary visibility for the backlit punctual contribution, preserving external-occluder suppression. Directional and unshadowed direct lighting use authored thickness; directional transmission retains ordinary shadow visibility. Thin sheets retain their authored exponential reflection/transmission partition. Solid transmittance uses the shared two-exponential diffusion profile and HDRP's long-tail adjustment for additional shadow thickness. Solid reflected and transmitted diffuse lighting both enter the diffusion signal; profile albedo is applied once during weighted composition. This prevents shadow-thickness sampling irregularities from bypassing diffusion as a sharp facial seam. Solid point/spot transmission also averages per-texel transmittance with a fixed nine-tap tent filter (1.5 shadow texels of support). Texel-center depth samples are converted to thickness before filtering; the filter stays within the light viewport and excludes clear-depth neighbors for a blocked center. An empty center retains authored-thickness transmission. This reduces shadow-thickness aliasing without increasing the material scattering distance; it adds shadow reads only to backlit solid transmission. The filter has no occluder identity information and remains a shadow-map approximation near overlapping geometry and cube-face boundaries. Specular and emission remain outside the diffuse filter.

Solid indirect transmission uses an additional raster back-face pass for standard, instanced and skinned meshes. It selects the nearest back-facing fragment behind the visible surface only when the render-instance ID matches, with alpha-mask rejection and mirrored-transform handling. An RGBA32F exit-depth/normal/validity buffer and a depth attachment are allocated only for cameras with active solid SSS. The GI query uses the reconstructed back-surface position and outward normal bias; front-screen AO is not applied to this hidden surface. Transmission uses the front-to-back displacement projected onto the front geometric normal, floored by authored thickness. This remains a camera-ray approximation for curved and concave geometry.

DDGI, SDFGI and HDDAGI gather at the back surface. HDDAGI uses its world-field gather for solid exits and the opposite-facing side of thin sheets, with its existing publication/coverage fallback and dynamic-receiver bias policy. This diffuse-only lookup does not trace sharp reflections or use front-screen AO. Thin sheets retain authored material AO. Missing exits (including unsupported external/strand geometry and clipped back surfaces) use environment irradiance with authored thickness. Disconnected components and overlapping particles within one render instance are not independently identified; this pass is not a general volume-intersection solver or full BSSRDF transport.

The camera inspector exposes **Raster SSS samples**, from 1 to 64, default 16. Screen-space diffusion cannot gather hidden/off-screen surfaces. The first quality target is comparable color, brightness, and scattering scale with PT while preserving frame stability. Reflection-probe capture currently uses the ordinary raster material response.

## Ray tracing and denoising

The shared integrator supports both RayQuery and Ray Tracing. Closed-solid sampling follows projected BSSRDF surface sampling with RGB-channel and projection-axis mixture PDFs and eligible-intersection selection. Probe support follows the reference's 99.9% radial quantile, so the small omitted profile tail is an approximation. SSS is evaluated only for primary direct lighting. Secondary surfaces use ordinary materials while retaining the authored optical interface, and ordinary surface indirect lighting is preserved. Thin sheets use cosine diffuse reflection/transmission lobes with energy partitioning.

Ray Tracing evaluates primary solid SSS in a dedicated closest-hit group. Raygen retains the iterative bounce loop, ordinary surface shading and thin-sheet response. Projection probes, visibility rays and secondary rays use the geometry-only group, so SSS cannot recursively invoke itself. Only SSS Ray Tracing pipelines use a 112-byte payload and recursion depth two; ordinary Ray Tracing keeps its 68-byte payload and depth one. RayQuery and ReSTIR retain the shared evaluator in their compute shaders. Scene variants without the subsurface feature compile out the solid diffusion sampler. NRD classifies SSS as diffuse transport, adjusts material demodulation for the profile albedo, and probes both reflection/transmission hemispheres for sheets. The pinned NRD normal packing has four material categories; SSS uses category 3. Exact per-profile spatial rejection is not provided by that category alone and remains a validation concern.

PT and ReSTIR select punctual/environment or emissive NEE at a primary solid subsurface point. Dedicated reuse applies to those NEE samples. A fresh one-hit BSDF estimator supplies complementary direct emitter/environment lighting; it does not trace bounced illumination from the sampled point. Indirect SSS transport and reuse are not supported.

**ReSTIR PT Candidate Only** on an SSS scene uses a dedicated RayQuery candidate path. It stores the primary solid-SSS point's triangle/barycentrics, sampling coordinates and intersection choice, selected lighting seeds, throughput and normalization in a 96-byte record, then reconstructs the point and light estimate for shading. Generation and resolve run in the same dispatch, independently for each SPP. The buffer retains the last SPP's record per pixel; it is not yet reused across frames or pixels. One buffer is allocated per active candidate camera (189.84 MiB at 1920×1080), counted in ray-camera memory statistics and released when leaving that mode.

**ReSTIR PT Spatial Only** and **Enhanced** now have experimental dedicated primary direct SSS reconnection. Spatial Only uses fresh canonical records; Enhanced first applies temporal reuse and then spatial reuse. Ordinary PT supplies transport outside the eligible primary solid-SSS estimate. These modes do not run surface-only ReSTIR shifts on SSS paths. Random-walk SSS remains an evaluation milestone rather than an exposed production mode.

Ordinary PT and the candidate path share the projected diffusion density evaluation, including the axis/channel mixture and surface-normal projection. This is one factor in the sampling weight; intersection selection, lighting proposals and reservoir selection still need separate treatment for cross-frame shifts. Temporal reconnection also evaluates the selected axis/channel component and its finite probe support.

**ReSTIR PT Temporal Only** now has an experimental dedicated RayQuery path for primary closed-solid direct SSS. It reconnects the subsurface point to the current receiver and reevaluates lighting, combines fresh/history estimates with reciprocal generalized balance MIS, and evaluates current visibility for the selected estimate. Ordinary PT supplies transport outside the primary solid-SSS estimate, including thin sheets. This does not enable ordinary surface-only ReSTIR shifts on SSS paths.

The eligible primary estimate is recorded during path generation and joined with the remaining transport before invalid-radiance rejection, sample averaging, accumulation and NRD signal scaling. Spatial modes retain a 64-byte residual per pixel to recompose the selected SSS estimate after neighbor reuse.

Two history buffers cost 442.97 MiB at 1920×1080, independent of SPP. Each history record is 112 bytes: bounded axis/light selectors share one word, and sampling seed, intersection ordinal and frame/pixel provenance are retained losslessly. All reused floats retain their original precision. Candidate Only uses its separate unchanged 96-byte encoding. Only the last sample per pixel reads and updates temporal SSS history; the remaining samples use fresh estimates. All samples contribute equally to the frame average. At 1 SPP this preserves the existing temporal estimator. Empty point/light draws count as trials. The camera's **Temporal history cap** bounds previous confidence; the adaptive duplication cap also applies to SSS through a dedicated 17×17 map of matching frame/pixel provenance. The optional shared map costs 7.91 MiB at 1080p. Receiver instance/material, normals, depth and finite sampling support govern reuse. Camera motion uses reprojection; scene geometry/lighting changes, including rigid object movement, discard SSS history. Material/profile edits, explicit camera resets, resize and mode changes also invalidate it.

Candidate Only and Temporal Only use scene-feature shader variants, with full-feature fallbacks. Their variants participate in startup shader preparation; activation resets camera history, and in-flight pipelines remain retained until GPU submission completes. Material features present in the scene, including thin transmission and volume, stay enabled. NRD signal generation is unchanged.

Spatial reuse reads immutable generated records and evaluates both reciprocal targets with the reference's defensive streaming pairwise MIS. Neighbors come from a uniform disk: twice the largest RGB scattering distance is projected to pixels and clamped to a 5–30 pixel radius. The existing spatial-neighbor control applies (default 3, maximum 16). Receivers must share object and material; finite probe support is evaluated separately, and eligible null samples remain trials. Only the last SPP uses spatial reuse; earlier SPP use fresh estimates.

A separate resolve composes eligible pixels' last sample unclamped residual and direct estimate before clamping, averaging and accumulation, then updates NRD signals. Ineligible pixels shade fresh PT inline and clear their SSS history before skipping resolve composition. Two 112-byte history buffers and one 64-byte residual buffer cost 569.53 MiB at 1920×1080. The residual buffer is camera-owned and reused with render-graph synchronization, without per-frame copies. Enhanced reuses the previous-history slot as spatial output after generation has consumed it; history advances once after resolve. Ineligible receivers and thin sheets retain fresh PT shading.

The default uses fixed-point reconnection with a unit Jacobian. Optional geometric delayed reconnection and the dedicated adaptive duplication map are described below. Surface Hybrid Shift does not apply to dedicated SSS samples. It has not established an equal-GPU-time quality win over ordinary PT. Use Candidate Only or ordinary PT for comparisons, and inspect raw output separately from NRD.

## Validation fixtures

### Human skin demo

Choose **Subsurface Scattering** in the launcher, or run `EvoEngineEditor.exe --demo sss --editor`. The demo uses the licensed Lee Perry-Smith male head sculpt, a uniform skin material, a shared `Skin.evesss` profile, and key/fill/rim lighting. It defaults to RayQuery, ordinary PT, and the standard REBLUR/NRD camera settings. The head is approximately 24 cm high; RGB diffusion distances are 1.2/0.5/0.25 mm. Exposure is fixed so material comparisons remain visible.

Select **SSS Male Head** and its mesh child, inspect its material, then adjust **Subsurface scattering → Weight** (0 for the surface-only comparison, 1 for the demo default), **Distance scale**, or the assigned profile. The profile is copied into the generated project's `Assets/Profiles/Skin.evesss`; saved edits survive restarts. Camera rendering modes and NRD can be changed using the existing camera inspector. The homogeneous preset is intended for visual evaluation rather than calibrated layered skin.

The studio point lights use a 2 m shadow distance for this small scene. Point-light shadow projection uses a near plane of distance/1000, and its bias is in projected depth units; adjust them together when changing the scene scale. An unsuitable range or bias can produce triangle-shaped shadow acne even with smooth mesh normals.

The head mesh and attribution are bundled under `Resources/SubsurfaceScattering`; see its README and original license. The generated project lives under `Resources/.generated/EvoEngine-DemoProjects/SubsurfaceScattering`. Closed-solid raster transmission is implemented; dedicated primary direct ReSTIR SSS is experimental. Ordinary PT remains the transport baseline for this demo.

For deterministic captures, `--demo sss --capture-demo-preview PATH --preview-sss-weight 0|1` overrides the skin weight for that capture without editing the profile. Keep the same camera, exposure, samples and denoising settings for on/off comparisons.

The [README gallery](../README.md#demo-gallery) links a native path-traced capture. Attribution and licensing are in
[the bundled asset guide](../Resources/SubsurfaceScattering/README.md). NRD uses running lobe averages with accumulation
enabled, including dedicated SSS reuse; see [denoising input policy](ray-camera-denoising.md).

### Sponza controls

Installed-editor captures can add controlled objects to Sponza using `--preview-sss-fixture mixed|solid|thin|disabled` together with `--capture-demo-preview`. Use `--preview-sss-weight 0|0.5|1` to override mesh-material weights after fixture creation for zero/partial/full-weight controls. Additional mixed fixtures `mixed-spot`, `mixed-directional`, `mixed-unshadowed` and `mixed-occluded` exercise transmission lighting and occluders. The mixed fixture includes a thick block, curved sphere, closed wedge, open paper sheet, and alpha-masked leaf, with front/back lighting. `disabled` creates matching geometry with zero SSS weight.

Use 1920x1080 captures, raw PT references, REBLUR/RELAX, and static/translating/rotating cameras. Report equal-SPP and equal-GPU-time comparisons separately. Implementation is not considered validated until the focused tests and installed-editor GPU captures are recorded.

`python Scripts/run_subsurface_validation.py` runs the installed-editor comparison matrix, including no-SSS denoiser controls and a 2048-sample static PT reference. Use `--pilot` for raster/raw-PT smoke captures or repeat `--case NAME` to select individual captures. Results include linear HDR images, GPU reports, and comparison metrics; the matrix does not establish an equal-GPU-time quality gate or independent physical correctness.

Use `--case energy-query --case energy-rtx --case energy-rtx-warm` for the isolated direct-light backend regression: Sponza 1080p, one surface bounce, full SSS weight, no denoising and 512 accumulated samples. It rejects an unlit solid and requires diffuse interior RGB means to agree within 3% on the block, sphere, wedge and paper. The sphere patch excludes its sharp specular highlight. The warm case repeats the capture in a second process using the same caches.

On 2026-10-16, 15 PT captures passed on the RTX 5070 with the specialized SSS closest-hit pipeline: one-bounce energy patch RGB differed from RayQuery by at most 0.00155%, partial/zero-weight patch means matched, and four-bounce specialized/full-feature RGB RMS was 0.01233%. REBLUR/TAA specialized/full-feature RGB RMS was 0.2033%. Four direct-SSS ReSTIR mode smoke captures also passed. Broader motion, material-boundary and denoiser quality acceptance remains open.

The previous raygen implementation intermittently lost or amplified solid-object energy, including with SER disabled. A matched raygen-placement control still failed a warm repeat. The dedicated closest-hit path passes these controls and retains scene specialization; the exact cause of the original failure remains undetermined.

Ray profile reports include the requested and active surface shader keys and readiness state. `--preview-full-ray-variant` selects the full-feature pipeline for controlled captures; ordinary runs use the scene-specific variant. A queued, missing fallback is pending, rather than ready.

### Native GI capture controls

Use `--preview-gi-provider sdfgi` or `--preview-gi-provider hddagi` with `--capture-demo-preview` to select the existing GI provider for a capture. `--preview-disable-ray-features` disables RT/RayQuery/acceleration structures for provider validation. The capture reports `EVOENGINE_PREVIEW_GI` and fails if the requested provider has not published valid lighting after the requested warmup. Use 1920x1080 Sponza captures with adequate GI warmup; provider readiness does not establish numerical convergence or absence of leakage.

The Sponza demo uses its imported floor; the duplicate white ground cube was removed because it competed with the textured floor for voxel albedo and caused large GI brightness variation across launches. Native 1920x1080 SDFGI/HDDAGI checks pass publication, no-RT operation and finite-output checks. Three independent mixed and zero-SSS captures per provider have less than 0.36% mean RGB spread. Small voxelization variation remains, and publication/repeatability does not establish physical energy correctness or absence of leakage.

The launcher lists the SSS head as the second demo; its stable CLI ID remains `sss`. PT and ReSTIR evaluate direct SSS only; indirect SSS has no camera setting or capture option.

## Geometric delayed reconnection

**SSS delayed reconnection** is an experimental default-off ReSTIR camera setting. It replays the stored SSS disk coordinates and intersection ordinal at the destination receiver, retains the selected light endpoint, and rejects a missing intersection. The reference BSSRDF-PDF ratio and light solid-angle reconnection Jacobian are applied with an explicit correction for the target's light-proposal-divided measure. Emissive triangles use an exit-independent area distribution; environment/directional and delta point/spot lights retain their corresponding fixed direction/endpoint. Finite-radius point/spot lights use fixed-point reuse. BSDF-hit lighting remains a fresh PT contribution outside the reused direct-light reservoir. Toggling it resets histories. Use `--preview-sss-delayed-reconnection enabled|disabled` for captures.

Additional Sponza fixtures: `mixed-masks` (0/0.5/1 weight bands and thickness ramp), `mixed-boundary` (adjacent sheet profiles), `mixed-mirrored`, `mixed-scale` (geometry/profile/thickness doubled), `mixed-geometry` (mirrored instances and a one-bone skinned sphere), `mixed-emissive` (area-light reconnection), and `mixed-energy` (isolated front directional lighting, environment/GI disabled and small solid diffusion distances). The scale fixture also scales point-light attenuation for matched local lighting. Captures can multiply shared profile distances once using `--preview-sss-profile-scale-after-frame FRAME SCALE`; edits are reported and exercise normal material/history invalidation.
Frame zero applies the profile edit before capture; later frames exercise edit invalidation during rendering.
The energy fixtures use an unshadowed front directional light to isolate the material response. `mixed-energy-scale` doubles the isolated fixture, authored distances and camera placement for a matched-light scale control; use camera position `0,0,2` looking at `0,0,-3`.

## Validation status

On 2026-10-14, Sponza 1920×1080 tests on an RTX 5070 passed for direct SSS PT on RayQuery and Ray Tracing, Candidate Only, Temporal Only, Spatial Only and Enhanced at 64 accumulated frames, 2 SPP and four bounces. Adaptive Temporal/Enhanced, delayed Enhanced, and Temporal with REBLUR plus NRD Temporal AA also completed with finite output. The previously reproducible device loss is no longer observed in these static tests. Reservoir replay reuses NEE samples; BSDF hits stay fresh, and geometry reconstruction remains outside the non-inlined lighting calculation. Candidate Only matched raw PT pixel-for-pixel in this fixture. Raw reuse image means were within 0.004% of matched PT; these static comparisons do not establish broader quality acceptance. Earlier no-SSS PT smoke controls also passed. These modes remain experimental: raw/REBLUR/RELAX motion/edit behavior and broad quality acceptance remain open. The later 2026-10-16 backend energy controls above passed; that narrower result does not establish general transport correctness.

Ray-camera shaders use high optimization for SPIR-V generation. SER compilation is limited to ray-generation shaders without linear swept spheres, matching the camera dispatch policy. Persistent shader and pipeline caches reduce repeat compilation. Pipeline cache files are bounded to 1 GiB. Source compilation and focused tests do not substitute for native rendering validation.
